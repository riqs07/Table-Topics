# Overnight audit: security, accessibility, performance, stickiness (2026-10-31)

**Purpose:** What was audited, what was changed and why, what was measured, and what is left for you to decide. Decision 156 points here.
**Scope:** The whole app as it stood on branch `claude/ecstatic-fermat-58t531` at commit 9663ce0 (60,000 lines of app code, 233 test files, 2,663 tests, all green before any change).
**How it was done:** Four read-only audits (security and user data, accessibility, performance, user experience and habit), then the fixes that were safe to make without you, each with tests, then lint, type check, the full unit suite, a production build and the browser suite on the final code. Two of the audit agents (performance and accessibility) were cut off by a usage limit part-way; both areas were finished by hand with measurements taken directly from the build and an axe scan of every page, so they are covered, but less exhaustively than security and UX.

## 1. The short version

- **Security:** no critical or high finding on the server. The gate, rate limits, input checks, headers and the service worker are careful and well tested. The real gaps were on the device: a backup file was only partly checked before import, and "Delete all my data" left the app's browser-storage notes behind. Both are fixed. Several smaller hardening items are done (time limits on every outside call, a body-size cap, two isolation headers, the image route off, a CI audit step). Four items need a product call and are listed in section 5.
- **Accessibility:** the app was already strong (skip link, focus on route change, labelled tabs, `aria-current`, reduced-motion handling, contrast tests, tap-target tests, a mic primer). An axe scan found three patterns: links whose spoken name dropped their visible words, pages without a level-one heading in some states, and one scrollable region not reachable by keyboard. All fixed, and the scan now runs in the browser suite over every page so it stays fixed.
- **Performance:** measured, not much changed. Every page ships about 560 KB of uncompressed JavaScript before its own code (React, Next.js and the shared screens), the home page 790 KB, the heaviest 1.3 MB; content is already split per route, the live-call library (618 KB) loads only on the conversation page, fonts swap, the service worker precaches with content hashes. The wins left are medium-sized refactors (section 5), not evening fixes.
- **Stickiness:** the daily loop, forgiving run, immediate celebration and kind copy are already the best-evidenced parts of the app. What was missing was small and structural: inside today's practice the second try was buried under "More options"; a worse second try was a bare number; progress sat two taps behind the gear; History's empty state sent people to Today instead of a take. Fixed. The bigger levers (a return trigger, an install nudge on Android, a short day) are product decisions, listed with evidence in section 5.

### 2.2 Accessibility

An axe scan (WCAG 2.0, 2.1, 2.2 A and AA plus best practices) now runs over every route in `tests/e2e/accessibility.spec.ts`; `@axe-core/playwright` was added as a dev dependency. Before the fixes it failed on 28 of 99 routes; after, it passes on all of them.

| Change | Why (criterion) | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Course and set cards: the progress ("3 of 16") is read after the visible name instead of an `aria-label` replacing the whole card | The spoken name left out the visible words (2.5.3 Label in Name): voice-control users saying the words on screen could not activate the card | `CourseCard.tsx` |
| Lesson rows, speech parts and the row and button links that took a `label`: the extra is read before or after the visible words, never instead of them | Same criterion; the labels for stories, 4/3/2 rows and the like duplicated what was visible and were dropped | `Buttons.tsx`, `SmallTalkScreen.tsx`, `SpeechScreen.tsx`, `StoriesScreen.tsx`, `FourThreeTwoScreen.tsx`, `TalkScreen.tsx`, `RehearseScreen.tsx` |
| A level-one heading in every state that lacked one: not-found states (session, talk, card, speech, mission, course, lesson, story), the scene and listening play states, a course part's try and wrap-up steps, the scenario round, Rehearse, the tile gallery | `RouteFocus` and the skip link land on `main h1`; without one, keyboard focus fell to the page itself and screen-reader users had no landmark heading (2.4.6, best practice) | the screens above, mostly an `sr-only` heading where the masthead already shows the title |
| The swipe deck's scrolling row is a labelled region with `tabIndex=0` | A scrollable region must be reachable by keyboard (2.1.1) | `SwipeDeck.tsx` |

Already done well, kept: skip link to the main heading; focus moved to the heading on each route change; `lang`; four labelled tabs with `aria-current`; tabs hidden during the task with a labelled close; keyboard hints only on devices with a hover pointer; `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured in animations and scrolling; a contrast test over every theme; a tap-target spec; live regions for recording state; the mic primer in the app's own words before the browser prompt.

Nothing in the data layer scans a whole table on render; housekeeping runs after the page shows.

### 2.4 User experience and stickiness

Evidence marks follow the playbook: **[S]** a study or platform guideline, **[A]** a practitioner framework or anecdote.

| Change | Why | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Inside today's practice, "Try it again" is a button right under the cue; the band still moves on to the next step | The PRD's loop is speak, one change, try again, see the change, with a 60% retry target; inside the plan the retry was a row under "More options" below the transcript toggle. Feedback helps when acted on at once [S, Kluger and DeNisi 1996]; cut steps to raise ability [A, Fogg]. Offered, never forced, as plan-daily-practice.md asks | `SessionScreen.tsx` |
| A second try whose number went the other way gets one task-focused line ("Second tries often pick up a few more pause words while the change is still in mind. The cue stands…"); the numbers stay | Person-focused or failure-framed feedback lowers persistence [S, Kluger and DeNisi; Silverman and Barasch 2023]; the playbook's own rule is never to end on a number that reads as failure | `focusMeasure.ts` (`retryNote`, tested), `SessionScreen.tsx` |
| History's empty state starts a free take instead of going to Today | An empty state should give the direct next action [A] | `HistoryScreen.tsx` |
| "See your progress" under the run line on Today, and a "Your progress" tile under Also today | Comparison with your past self is the PRD's motivation lever, but Profile and History were two or three taps behind the gear | `HomeScreen.tsx` |
| Two copy leaks fixed: a repository path shown to the user, and "Add the right passphrase" without saying where | Setup details are not interface (playbook 6a) | `feedbackMessages.ts` |
| Manifest shortcuts (Today's practice, Rapid Fire) | Chrome's richer install surface uses them [S, web.dev] | `manifest.ts` |
| The offline page wears the app's paper colour, a heading and a button home | It was unstyled default HTML | `sw.template.js` |

Already strong, kept: first value in three taps with no account or passphrase; the mic primer; one change and one cue per take with a local fallback; a forgiving run that never shows a break and says "Welcome back"; tick-off and once-a-day celebration; "Your first one!" and personal bests; kind self-review framing; comparison with past self everywhere; "Can't speak now?" without penalty; four labelled tabs hidden during the task.

## 5. Not changed: for you to decide

Ordered by how much I would do them first.

1. **A return trigger.** Nothing brings people back: no push, no in-app reminder, only a calendar file in Settings. iOS web push works for installed apps since 16.4 with a user gesture [S, Apple]. Duolingo's smarter reminders moved new-user retention by about 2% [S, Yancey and Settles 2020], so expect modest, but today there is none. Needs a push server and a stored subscription (decision 2's known risk; plan-launch-and-scale.md already lists it). Default off, one a day, stop after three unanswered.
2. **A cross-platform install nudge** after the first finished day: listen for `beforeinstallprompt`, show one line on Today, respect "Not now" with the existing quiet days. Today only iOS Safari gets a nudge, framed as data safety. [S, web.dev]
3. **A lighter day one.** Picking a focus adds the course step, making day one five steps and the headline "A few short steps"; sticky users of a comparable app practise about four minutes a day [S, Calm, PMC6928287]. Drop the course step when nothing has been done yet, or move it to "Keep going".
4. **A short day and a lighter first day back** after more than a week: keep the first speaking step, mark the rest skipped. One missed day does not hurt habit formation; quitting does [S, Lally 2010].
5. **Record without live words when the speech service is offline:** audio plus on-device pauses and pace, with a plain note. Today it is a dead end.
6. **Welcome: Start above the fold on a phone.** The drawing, two paragraphs and six one-column focus tiles push Start to about 950 px. A two-column picker or a smaller drawing would fix it; check with a 375 px screenshot first.
7. **Security, needing a design call:** the passphrase sits in plain `localStorage`, reachable by any script that ever got in (an `HttpOnly` cookie set by a small route would take it out of reach); a dishonest client can refund its live minutes by reporting `seconds: 0` (record the start server-side and refund `min(reported, elapsed)`); counter expiry is two calls, so a failed `EXPIRE` leaves a counter for good (use the store's pipeline); `clientAddress` trusts the first `X-Forwarded-For` entry, safe on Vercel, not elsewhere; CI actions are pinned by major tag, not commit.
8. **Performance, medium refactors:** move the shared zod schemas out of the browser bundle; load only the current theme's fonts; consider `zod/mini`.
9. **Copy:** "Feedback is paused. {reason}" shows the server's reason verbatim; it names settings, never values, which the playbook asks for, so it stays, but read it once as a stranger would.
