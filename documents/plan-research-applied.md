# Plan: putting the charisma and connection research to work

Proposed and started 2026-10-18, at the user's request ("write a plan doc and then execute"). It applies [research/charisma-connection-deep-dive-2026-10.md](research/charisma-connection-deep-dive-2026-10.md) to the whole app. The research in one line: **warmth is a set of small, teachable moves that people undervalue, and practice with specific feedback plus real-world tries is what changes behaviour; reading and choosing alone do not.**

## Principles every stage follows

1. **Every lesson ends in doing.** Read → recognise → say it aloud → try it for real → compare with what you expected → review later in a new situation.
2. **Feedback is about the task, never the person.** "You asked three questions in a row; try one about their answer", never "you seemed nervous". (Over a third of feedback interventions made performance worse, mostly self-focused ones; Kluger & DeNisi 1996.)
3. **Rules, not scripts.** Every model answer comes with the move behind it, and the learner says it in their own words.
4. **"Your fear is miscalibrated" is a core message.** Compliments, deeper talk, vulnerability and being liked all go better than people expect.
5. **Honest labels.** Evidence strength is shown in words; myths are named; scenario questions are called a warm-up, not training on their own.
6. **No penalties.** No hearts, lives or leaderboards. A misstep is the most useful moment in a lesson.

## Stage 1: Missions with predict, do, compare (Connect, Today, History)

The biggest research-backed gap. In the week-long talk-to-strangers study, people who did missions became less afraid of rejection; people who only watched did not.

- **Content:** a ladder of real-world missions, easy to harder, each tied to a move (follow-up, listening, good news, compliments, thanks, ending well, deeper questions, names as memory). Each has one **safety behaviour to drop** ("don't rehearse your next line; listen to theirs") and the Learn card behind it.
- **Morning (accept):** the mission card on Today (after the steps) and on Connect: what to try, the habit to drop, then "How do you think it will go?" (1 to 5) and "I'll try it", or "Not today".
- **Evening (debrief), by taps and by voice:** Did you try it? How did it go (1 to 5)? What did you notice? (a short voice note or typed line). Then the compare: "You expected 2 of 5. It went 4 of 5."
- **Climbing:** the next mission is the first not yet tried. "Too much" on a debrief steps back to an easier one.
- **Over time:** Connect shows predicted against actual for recent missions, so "it goes better than I fear" becomes visible.
- **Counts:** a debriefed mission counts for the streak and the week strip, and is listed in History under "Real conversations".
- **Storage:** with the settings (`missions`), like Learn progress, so it travels in exports. Debrief notes are text only and never sent anywhere.

## Stage 2: Feedback rules in every coach

- Add explicit rules to all three coach prompts (speech, small talk, follow-ups and listening): comment on what was said, never on the person's character, confidence or nerves; name the move behind any model answer.
- The follow-up and listening coach scores against **responsiveness**: did the reply show they were **understood**, that their reaction **made sense**, and that you **care** what happens next?
- Audit the phone's own "one change" texts for person-focused wording.

## Stage 3: Learn — a "Warmth and connection" set and scenario questions

- **New cards** (from the research): good news needs a real reaction; understood, validated, cared for; share then hand it back; practice changes you, reading doesn't; the compliment you hold back; thanks with a reason; hold the advice; "small talk makes you unhappy" (myth); fast replies come from listening; "be funny" is not the goal.
- **Card fixes:** mirroring cites the preregistered null; names says plainly that using names to be liked is unsupported (a memory habit, not a trick); the two parts of charisma adds "warmth before expertise in first meetings".
- **Scenario questions** ("choose, then say it"): a short situation, three or four replies (each wrong one a named, tempting mistake), a reveal with tiers (best, also good, flat, misstep) and the move behind it, then "say it in your own words". Cards in the new set end with one; the Learn tab has a quick-scenarios practice of three; a missed move returns later in a new situation. Items keyed by more than one person before they are relied on (the first eight come from the research report).
- **"Your fear vs reality"** wording on the undervalued moves.

## Stage 4: Connect — a "Responding to news" unit

- Good news gets a reaction and a question about the moment; bad news gets understanding before advice.
- Beats: React, Ask about it, Stay with them, No buts (no downside, no shift, no advice unasked).
- Three lessons and a bank of news (good and bad); the phone checks the beats; the conversation coach covers the unit.
- Later units (reading the room, starting and ending, going deeper) follow the research: notice changes and pay attention rather than copy body language; a depth ladder (fact → opinion → feeling → meaning, up one rung at most); remember one name and use it when you say goodbye.

## Stage 5 (later): the live conversation partner

- A partner that talks and a separate coach afterwards (personalised feedback after role-play beat practice alone in a 2026 trial).
- The partner shares too, so you practise the back-and-forth, not an interview; it varies, remembers, and does not interrupt or always agree (the common complaints about current AI partners).
- Notes after: talk share, follow-ups, shifts to yourself, coming back to what they said; one change; try again.

## Stage 6 (later): measure transfer honestly

- History's "Real conversations": missions tried, predicted against actual, and first-try accuracy on scenarios you have not seen, per move.
- "How this works" says plainly what is a warm-up and what does the work.
