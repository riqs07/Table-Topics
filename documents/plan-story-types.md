# Plan: stories you'll need, and the guided-story dial

**Built 2026-10-30 (decisions 152, 153 and 154).** Turns [research/storytelling-2026-10.md](research/storytelling-2026-10.md) into the story mode. The user asked to "go ahead and let it rip": create the modes, put them under the Stories group on Practice, add them to the bank Today picks from, and give each a blob scene.

## The idea in one line

The app trained everyday anecdotes well but not the handful of stories people tell again and again and dread: **who I am**, **an interview answer**, **a toast** and **a choice I made**. They become templates with their own prompts and checks. And the guided stories, where the details are given, become a five-step dial that ends on those same types.

## What the research says, and what it changes

| Finding (from the storytelling report) | Strength | What the app does |
|---|---|---|
| Every good short story shares one skeleton: a normal, a break, a chain of causes, a moment of change, and why it mattered | Moderate (Labov) to practical | Every new template keeps that skeleton, with beats named for its job |
| Adults need a few high-stakes story types the app lacked: who-I-am, STAR interview answers, toasts, values stories | Practical (Simmons, Hall, STAR guides); priority is judgement | Four new templates, six prompts each, in their own prompt groups |
| A STAR answer spends most of its time on Action and Result, says "I", and has a measurable result | Practical (interview guides) | The phone checks "I" against "we", a number, and a closing reflection |
| A toast tells one story that shows one quality, then raises a glass; 3 to 5 minutes at most | Practical | The phone checks for the story, the quality and the raised glass |
| Constraints help creativity, and the effect lasts after the constraint goes (Haught-Tromp); too many hurt | Strong to moderate | Guided stories keep three to five details at every level |
| Causally linked material is easier to tell; shuffled or loose material is harder | Moderate (L2 learners; native speakers less affected) | Three new levels make the dial: shuffled facts, loose things to connect, and loose things with a required type |
| One goal and one next step per telling | Strong (Hattie and Timperley) | Each new type gives two or three checks, not a long list; the coach gets one line on what the type needs |

## The four types (decision 152)

| Type | For | Beats | Phone checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who I am** | "Tell me about yourself", meeting people, the start of a talk | A moment from before · What it showed me · How it led to now | Starts from a moment, not a list · Links it to now · A feeling or a reason |
| **Interview answer** | "Tell me about a time when…" | The situation · My task · What I did · The result · What I'd keep | Says what *you* did ("I" at least as often as "we") · A result you can measure (a number) · Ends on what you'd keep or learned |
| **Toast** | Weddings, leaving drinks, a birthday, thanking someone | How I know them · A story that shows them · What it says about them · Raise a glass | One moment with them, told · Says what makes them who they are · Ends by raising a glass |
| **A choice I made** | Values questions, leadership, "a time you stood up for something" | The pressure · The choice · What it cost · Why it mattered | Names the choice · Says what it cost · Says why it mattered |

Each has a short worked example (as the first four templates do), a coach note on what the type needs, six prompts in its own group on the Stories page ("Who you are", "Interview questions", "Toasts and thank-yous", "A choice you made"), and a blob scene. The setup screen shows the templates in two families, everyday and "Stories you'll need", with the chosen one's family first and "Show all templates" for the rest; "How long?" moves above the example so the Start band no longer covers it.

## The guided-story dial (decision 153)

| Level | Name | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Just the facts | Plain facts in order (existing) |
| 2 | With a twist | Facts that end on a twist (existing) |
| 3 | **Shuffled** | The facts, out of order: find the story in them |
| 4 | **Connect these** (Story dice) | Four things that don't belong together: someone, a place, an object, a problem |
| 5 | **Make it a…** | Four things, and the kind of story to tell: a toast, an interview answer, who I am, a choice |

The checks are the guided story's own: every detail heard, and what was added beyond them. Level 5 also sets the template, so the type's checks and beats apply.
