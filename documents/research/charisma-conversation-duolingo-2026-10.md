# Research: charisma, conversation, body language, and how Duolingo works (2026-10-18)

Asked for by the user before planning the daily mix, the conversation curriculum (asking, listening, reading the room) and an educational part of the app. Each finding notes how strong the evidence is. Sources are linked; several are secondary write-ups, which is said where it matters.

## 1. Charisma

**What it is.** Research on everyday charisma splits it into two parts: **influence** (presence, leading, swaying people) and **affability** (making people comfortable, friendliness, getting along with anyone). Influence goes with being seen as competent; affability with being seen as warm. Charisma measured this way predicted how persuasive speakers were ([Tskhay, Zhu, Zou & Rule, JPSP 2018, summarised by Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com:443/us/blog/the-attraction-doctor/202312/the-science-of-charisma-for-romance-work-and-everyday-life)). *Moderate evidence; one research group's model.*

**It can be trained.** Managers taught a set of "charismatic leadership tactics" (stories, contrasts, rhetorical questions, lists of three, showing conviction, gestures, voice) in a short workshop, then given practice speeches with feedback, were rated more charismatic, a medium-sized effect ([Antonakis, Fenley & Liechti 2011, via BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/charisma-involves-teachable-behaviours); [summary](https://siliconcanals.com/t-research-led-by-john-antonakis-at-the-university-of-lausanne-found-that-targeted-training-produced-a-medium-improvement-in-how-charismatic-people-appeared-to-others-evidence-that-charisma-is-n/)). The gains came from observable behaviours practised with feedback, not from reading about them. *Moderate evidence; this is close to what VoiceLab already does.*

**The voice matters.** Listeners rated speakers with more pitch variation, a slightly higher pitch and a faster rate as more charismatic, and prosody training improved scores ([Berger, Niebuhr & Peters 2017](https://pub.dega-akustik.de/DAGA_2017/data/articles/000256.pdf); [Niebuhr et al. 2020, Jobs vs. Zuckerberg](https://doaj.org/article/ab437b55da8848689a04948dba9d8524); [training study 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2019.00012/pdf)). *Moderate; culture-dependent.*

## 2. Body language: what holds up and what does not

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|-------|---------|--------|
| "93% of communication is non-verbal" (7-38-55) | **Myth.** From narrow lab studies on liking, with single words; Mehrabian himself says it does not apply to communication in general | [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/the-learning-curve/the-7-38-55-rule-debunking-the-golden-ratio-of-conversation), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Mehrabian) |
| Power posing raises confidence hormones | **Did not replicate.** A larger study found no hormone or behaviour effect; the original lead author no longer believes in it. Feeling a bit more powerful may remain | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_posing), [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2016/09/26/yes-power-pose-study-is-flawed-but-shouldnt-be-retracted-says-one-author/) |
| You can read emotions from facial expressions | **Weak.** A major review found facial movements are not reliable or specific signs of particular emotions; context matters a lot | [Barrett et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6640856/) |
| You can spot liars from body language | **No.** People are right 54% of the time (chance is 50%); experts do no better | [Bond & DePaulo 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/) |
| Genuine ("Duchenne", eye-crinkling) smiles come across better | **Yes, as perceived:** rated more genuine and trustworthy, though people can fake them | [Gunnery & Ruben 2016](https://api.philpapers.org/rec/GUNPOD), [BPS](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/most-people-can-fake-genuine-duchenne-smile) |
| Mirroring people makes them like you | **Mixed.** Original finding partly replicated (smiling yes, other mannerisms no); can backfire if noticed | [Replication atlas](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1037/0022-3514.76.6.893/) |
| Eye contact: more is better | **More nuanced.** Mutual eye contact comes at peaks of shared attention, then breaks so each person can add something new; it rises and falls naturally | [Wohltjen & Wheatley 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449315) |
| First impressions from seconds of behaviour | **Real but risky.** Thin slices of nonverbal behaviour predict some outcomes; Ambady warned they are also where stereotypes do harm | [Harvard Magazine](https://harvardmagazine.com/2001/07/snap-judgments-work-html) |

**What to teach instead of "decoding".** Notice *changes* in a person (they light up, answers get shorter, they glance away more), treat them as questions rather than answers, and check by asking or adjusting. Use your own face and voice to show interest: a real smile when something is funny, nodding, "mm" and specific reactions (see listening).

## 3. Conversation: findings for the curriculum

**People like you more than you think.** After talking with strangers, people consistently underestimated how much their partner liked them, in the lab, among new roommates and at workshops. Observers could see the liking; the speakers were too busy with their own worries to notice it ([Boothby et al. 2018](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/The-Liking-Gap-in-Conversations.pdf)). *Strong; a good confidence lesson.*

**Deeper talk goes better than expected** ([Kardas, Kumar & Epley](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/deep-conversations-strangers-are-more-fulfilling-small-talk); from the small-talk research).

**Asking.**
- Follow-up questions make people better liked, through feeling listened to ([Huang et al. 2017](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/it-helps-ask-cumulative-benefits-asking-follow-questions)).
- **"Boomerasking"** (asking a question only to answer it yourself, "How was your weekend? Mine was amazing...") makes people seem less sincere and less likeable, though most people think it helps. Ask questions you cannot answer yourself, and stay with their answer one more turn ([Brooks & Yeomans 2025, via BPS](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/improve-your-conversation-skills-ditch-boomerasking); [Imperial](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/business-school/ib-knowledge/management/boomerasking-how-break-the-annoying-habit-hurting-your-workplace/)). *Recent, strong design.*
- Pick up "free information": the details people mention in passing are the easiest follow-ups ([Art of Manliness on Alan Garner](https://www.artofmanliness.com/social/social-skills/social-briefing-9-think-questions-ask-people/)).

**Listening.**
- Listeners are **co-narrators**: when listeners were distracted and stopped giving specific reactions (wincing, "no way!"), storytellers told their stories worse, especially the endings. Generic "mm-hmm" and nods help; specific reactions help more ([Bavelas, Coates & Johnson 2000](https://www.responsebasedpractice.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Listeners-as-Co-Narrators.pdf); [timing, 2002](https://talkbank.org/ca/access/0docs/bavelas2002.pdf)). *Strong.*
- High-quality listening (attentive, empathic, not judging) makes speakers less anxious and clearer about their own views ([Itzchakov, Kluger et al. 2018](https://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~kgdemarr/Pub/2018.IDKT.PSPB.pdf)).
- **Paraphrasing:** mixed. One study found paraphrasing made listeners seem more socially attractive but did not make people feel more understood; a later one found active listening beat advice for feeling understood ([Weger et al. 2010](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/scopus2010/1477); [2014 study](https://fellow.app/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/The-Relative-Effectiveness-of-Active-Listening-in-Initial-Interactions-1.pdf)). Teach it as checking you understood, not a formula.
- **Support vs. shift** responses: keeping attention on their topic rather than turning it to yourself ([Derber's conversational narcissism, summarised](https://philosophicalsociety.com/Archives/Conversational%20Narcissism.htm)). *Older, observational; matches the boomerasking findings.*

**Turn-taking.** Across 10 languages people avoid overlapping and keep gaps between turns short, with differences between cultures ([Stivers et al. 2009](https://forms.mpi.nl/node/50939)). Background only.

**Ending.** Conversations almost never end when both people want them to; the average gap between wanted and actual length was about half the conversation, and people cannot tell when their partner wants to stop ([Mastroianni et al. 2021, Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2021/03/researchers-find-conversations-dont-end-when-people-want-them-to/)). *Strong; supports teaching warm exits.*

**Practising in real life.** Graded steps from easy to harder (smile at someone, small talk with a cashier, introduce yourself to a group) and reviewing each attempt for what you learned rather than success are standard in CBT for social anxiety ([Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/cbt-for-social-anxiety.html)). *Clinical practice; the sources found were practitioner guides, not trials.* Fits missions and debriefs.

## 4. How Duolingo works, and what fits VoiceLab

| Duolingo | Evidence | For VoiceLab |
|----------|----------|--------------|
| **A path of units**, each lesson mixing new material with earlier material due for review (spacing) | Its scheduling model raised retention 9.5% in its own A/B test ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/spaced-repetition-for-learning); [Settles & Meeder 2016](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/P16-1174.pdf)) | **Yes:** a path of units for each area, with review of earlier lessons mixed into the daily steps |
| **Short lessons** (about 5 minutes) | Design commentary only | **Already:** daily practice is 5 to 10 minutes |
| **Streaks** and the streak-saver reminder | 10-day streak users dropped off less (correlation); the reminder was its first big retention win ([growth write-up](https://marishalakhiani.substack.com/p/breaking-down-duolingos-growth-model)) | **Yes, kindly:** a forgiving streak (rest days) and an optional reminder, later |
| **XP and weekly leagues** (30 random learners) | Engagement, not learning ([mechanics](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/leagues)) | **No:** a personal app; comparison adds pressure without teaching |
| **Hearts** (lose one per mistake) | No learning evidence | **No:** penalising mistakes works against speaking anxiety |
| **AI video calls and role-play (Lily)**: no corrections mid-call, feedback after, transcript, a character with personality, remembers small details | Product design ([Duolingo Max](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-max); [Video Call](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-launches-ai-powered-video-call-android)); reviews call conversations short and scripted ([review](https://copycatcafe.com/blog/duolingo-max)) | **Yes, for the live conversation partner:** characters, feedback after, a transcript, no mid-conversation correction |
| **"Explain my answer"**: why something was right or wrong | Product feature ([StoryLearning](https://storylearning.com/duolingo-max-review)) | **Yes:** the "why it works" lines and the educational cards |

**Learning science behind it** (secondary summaries, consistent with the wider literature): recalling something beats rereading it; spacing beats cramming; mixing kinds of practice (interleaving) helps long-term retention even though it feels harder; feedback strengthens learning ([summary](https://www.sec-ed.co.uk/content/best-practice/a-teacher-s-guide-to-retrieval-practice-interleaving); [interleaving study](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10250915)). The three-lane daily mix is interleaving; short recall questions in the educational cards are retrieval practice.

## 5. Speaking, nerves and memory (added for the Learn cards)

- **The spotlight effect:** people wearing an embarrassing T-shirt guessed about twice as many others noticed it as actually did ([Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky 2000, summarised by Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/between-you-and-me/201311/have-you-fallen-prey-to-the-spotlight-effect)). *Strong, classic.*
- **The illusion of transparency:** speakers think their nerves show far more than they do; speakers told about this felt calmer and were rated as giving better speeches ([Savitsky & Gilovich 2003, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illusion_of_transparency)). *Moderate; a single study, but useful and harmless.*
- **"I'm excited" instead of "I'm calm":** saying it raised reported excitement; a 2025 direct replication confirmed the feeling but found no difference in how the speeches were rated ([Poynter & Pasqualini 2025](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.58734/plc-2025-0011.pdf); original described by the [APA](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2013/12/performance-anxiety)). *Mixed: it may help how you feel, not how you come across.*
- **"Uh" and "um" are words:** they signal a delay is coming, and listeners recognised the next word faster after "uh" ([Clark & Fox Tree 2002, and Fox Tree's listener studies, summarised](https://sciencenotes.ucsc.edu/0701/talk); [later work](https://www.cstr.ed.ac.uk/downloads/publications/2014/Dall_Wester_Corley_IS14.pdf)). *Moderate; a few fillers are normal, many are distracting.*
- **Names:** linking a name to meaning works better than just repeating it, and recalling it later (self-testing) beats hearing it again ([Psychology in Action](https://www.psychologyinaction.org/2022-10-24-how-can-we-improve-our-memory-of-names)). *Moderate.*
- **Stories:** being absorbed in a story ("transportation") goes with being persuaded by it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_theory_(psychology))), but a large experiment found stories no more persuasive than a list of facts for a policy question ([Schreiner et al.](https://www.mcm.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06110300/user_upload/Publikationen/Schreiner_Appel_Isberner_Richter_PrePrint.pdf) and the climate study cited in [this review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5954649/)). *Mixed: stories help people stay with you; they are not magic.*

## 6. What this means for the plan

1. Teach charisma as **behaviours you can practise with feedback**: presence and warmth, stories, voice variety. Not a personality.
2. Teach body language as **noticing and showing**, not decoding, and say plainly which popular claims are myths. That honesty is itself good educational content.
3. The conversation units have solid findings to build on: follow-up questions, avoiding boomerasking, co-narrating as a listener, the liking gap, and warm exits.
4. Borrow Duolingo's path, short lessons, spaced review, a kind streak and feedback after conversations; leave out leagues and hearts.
5. Missions should climb from easy to harder, with debriefs about what was learned, not whether it went well.
